# faster-than-light-memes

Benjamin Schwerdtner's blog. See README.org for the build.

## Generated text

- Whenever Claude writes text for a post or page, mark it at the very bottom of that post, never at the top:

  ```org
  -----

  /Generated by Claude on Benjamin Schwerdtner's behalf./
  ```

  In German posts: `/Generiert von Claude im Auftrag von Benjamin Schwerdtner./`
- Mark it even when only part of the post is generated. No "draft" markers at the top instead.

## Writing style

- Take inspiration from Benjamin's own posts in =posts/= so that his spirit shines through, not a generic essay voice:
  curious and playful, short punchy lines, bold claims followed by "but is it?",
  coined terms (/neuronal animism/, /one idea mode/), references in brackets [Deutsch], [Braitenberg], [Hofstadter],
  thinking out loud, real questions he does not know the answer to.
- But output what an LLM is good at: well-formatted prose (not raw note bullets), with anecdotes, concrete examples,
  small stories, analogies and jokes where they help the idea land.
- Lists of open questions are good. Use them generously where the honest state is "I don't know yet".
- Keep his own sentences where they work; fill in around them rather than replacing his voice.

## Factual claims

- Every factual claim in generated text gets a `[verify]` marker right after it.
- Each marked claim needs a basis in the evidence: what the studies/sources say, with links, as an org footnote next to the marker:

  ```org
  Hypnosis makes people recall more, but much of the extra is wrong. [verify][fn:hypnosis]

  [fn:hypnosis] Dywan & Bowers 1983 (J. Abnorm. Psych.): more recall, more errors, confidence up regardless. <link>
  ```

- Get that basis from a verifier agent, one claim (or a small group) per agent: it searches, gives a verdict, the sources, and its criticism of the claim as written (overstated, misattributed, contested, weak sample...). Put that criticism into the footnote; don't hide it.
- If the verifier answered from memory instead of searching, say so in the footnote.
- Once a claim holds (verdict TRUE or MOSTLY TRUE after any fixes, and the verifier actually searched), remove its `[verify]` marker and keep the footnote. The marker stays on claims that are unchecked, checked only from memory, or that failed.
